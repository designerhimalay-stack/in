Generative AI Use Cases: 12 That Mid-Sized Indian Companies Can Put Into Production

Move beyond AI pilots. Explore 12 practical generative AI use cases mid-sized Indian companies can deploy into production right now.

12 November 2026·13 min read

The generative AI use cases that mid-sized Indian companies get into production first are narrow ones: one team, one set of documents, and one task someone already does by hand every day. Plant SOP assistants and vendor invoice matching usually lead. Their data already exists, and the person checking the AI’s output is the same person who does that work today.

Most companies we speak with are not starting from zero. Nearly half of Indian enterprises now have more than one generative AI use case live in production (EY and CII, 2025). Another 23% are still in pilots. That second group is the one I recognize from almost every discovery call: someone built a promising demo in a quarter, showed it to the MD, and it never left the sandbox.

Those pilots rarely stall because of the model. They stall on messy documents and missing access rules, and on one question nobody asked at the start: who checks the answer before it reaches a customer?

Money is tight as well. More than 95% of organizations in the same survey spend less than a fifth of their IT budget on AI and machine learning (EY and CII, 2025). So a use case has to earn its place against the ERP upgrade and the next laptop refresh. Each entry below says what it runs on and how hard it is to start, so you can judge that for yourself.

Which generative AI use cases reach production fastest?

Use cases built on documents you already control reach production fastest, because nothing has to be integrated before the first test. Ones that write into your ERP or reply to customers take longer. They need integration work and guardrails, plus sign-off from whoever owns that system.

The 12 generative AI use cases in detail

1. Shop-floor SOP assistant

A shift supervisor in Chakan needs the torque setting for a fixture change on line 3. Today she walks to the quality office, or phones the one person who remembers. With an SOP assistant, she asks on her phone in Marathi or English and gets the answer from the approved SOP, with the document number shown underneath.

Underneath, this is retrieval-augmented generation: the model answers only from documents you load into it, and what each person can see follows their role. A contract worker sees line SOPs and nothing from HR. The catch is version control. If three copies of the same SOP sit in SharePoint, the assistant will quote whichever it finds first, so name one owner for SOP versions before you build anything.

2. Vendor invoice and purchase order matching

Vendor invoices land in the accounts@ inbox as PDFs and scans, plus the odd phone photo of a printout. A generative model reads each one and pulls out the supplier’s GST number and line items. It then matches them against the purchase order and goods receipt in your enterprise resource planning system, whether that runs on Tally or Dynamics 365 Business Central.

Clean matches go through. Mismatches go to a person, and that is deliberate. The model reads a smudged scan well, but it should not decide whether a short shipment gets paid. This is also one of the easiest use cases to put a rupee value on, since your team already knows how many invoices it handles each month.

3. RFQ responses and technical quotations

For engineering products firms, a customer’s request for quotation can run to 40 pages of specifications. The estimator’s first two days often go into reading clauses and copying answers from old quotations.

Generative AI can read the specification and find the closest past quotations. From those, it drafts a clause-by-clause compliance statement, each clause marked as comply or deviate, for the estimator to review. Pricing stays with the estimator. My advice is to start with the compliance statement alone, because it is the most tedious part of the job and the easiest one to check.

4. Maintenance history assistant

Breakdown registers are full of entries like “bearing noise, replaced, ok”. One entry tells you little. Three years of them tell you which machine keeps failing and which spare keeps running out.

An assistant reads that free text from your maintenance system or scanned registers and gives the technician a short history before the job starts: the last few failures on that machine and what fixed each one. It will not predict the next failure on its own. That takes sensor data and a machine learning model, and the cleaned-up maintenance history from this use case is often the first input that model gets.

5. Quality complaint and CAPA drafting

When an automotive customer raises a quality complaint, the clock starts on the corrective and preventive action report. The quality engineer pulls inspection records and past complaints on the same part, then writes it up against the customer’s deadline.

Generative AI drafts the problem description and surfaces the closest past complaints, including ones filed under a different part number. Root cause analysis stays with the engineer. Nobody should sign a CAPA report that a model wrote.

6. Dealer and distributor query assistant

Ask a sales coordinator at a mid-sized manufacturer where the day goes, and a good part of it is dealers asking the same questions on WhatsApp. Is this model in stock at the Nagpur depot? Which scheme applies this month? Where is my order?

An assistant connected through the WhatsApp Business Platform can answer these in Hindi or English from live ERP stock and the current scheme circulars. Set firm limits. It can quote stock and published schemes, it should never agree to a discount, and anything outside its scope goes to a named person with the conversation attached.

7. Customer service email triage and reply drafts

Budgets are already moving here: 54% of Indian enterprises expect to increase AI spending on customer service over the next year (EY and CII, 2025). The practical version is modest. The model reads each incoming email and tags it by type. It pulls out the order number, then drafts a reply from your service policy for an agent to edit and send.

Start with drafts, not auto-sending. Once agents have accepted most drafts with light edits for a few weeks, you have the evidence to automate the simplest categories.

8. Month-end MIS commentary for finance

The numbers are ready in Power BI by the fourth working day. The commentary is not. Someone in finance still writes “power and fuel cost rose because…” dozens of times for the management pack, usually late in the evening.

Generative AI can draft variance commentary from the figures in your finance system and flag the movements worth a sentence. The rule we hold to is simple: the model only explains numbers it is given. It never fetches or estimates a figure itself, and a finance analyst checks every line against the ledger before the pack reaches the chief financial officer.

9. Contract and vendor agreement review

Most mid-sized firms have a standard vendor agreement and a pile of signed versions that drifted away from it. A review assistant compares each incoming contract with your template and highlights where payment terms or liability caps differ.

It can also flag clauses on personal data, which deserve closer attention as the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its rules phase in. Legal still decides. What changes is the starting point: a marked-up comparison instead of a blank first read.

10. HR and IT helpdesk in Microsoft Teams

Appraisal season brings the same questions to HR every year, and so does the week after any policy change. An assistant built in Microsoft Copilot Studio can answer leave and reimbursement questions, or walk someone through a Provident Fund withdrawal, using the policies already on SharePoint. Staff ask inside Microsoft Teams, where they already work.

IT gets the same relief on password resets and VPN steps. If you already pay for Microsoft 365, this is often the cheapest place to start, though the answers are only as current as the policy documents behind them. See how we approach Microsoft Copilot rollouts.

11. Legacy code documentation

For established technology firms, and for in-house IT teams at manufacturers, the quiet win is documentation. Almost every company has a VB6 or older .NET application that runs something important and that only one person fully understands.

Generative AI can read that code and explain what each module does. It can draft test cases too, before anyone changes a line. This pays off most when legacy modernization is on the roadmap, because the team that documents first hits fewer surprises during migration.

12. Plain-English questions on business data

A sales head wants to know which dealers in Gujarat ordered less this quarter than last. Today that is a request to the MIS team and a two-day wait. With Copilot in Power BI or a Microsoft Fabric data agent, she types the question and gets the answer from your governed data model.

Leaders ask for this one most, and it depends most on groundwork. If “dealer” means something different in each of your reports, the answer will be wrong and will sound sure of itself. Most of the effort goes into data engineering and the semantic model, not the AI.

What has to be true before a generative AI use case goes live?

A use case is ready for production when someone owns the source data and you can measure the answers against real examples. You also need a person who checks the output at a fixed point, and a cost per transaction in rupees that finance accepts.

  1. A named data owner. One person decides which SOP, policy, or price list is current. Without that, the assistant quotes outdated documents with full confidence.
  2. A test set built before the first prompt. Collect 50 to 100 real questions or documents with known right answers. My favorite part of any engagement is the first run against this set, because it ends the debate about whether the pilot “feels” accurate.
  3. A defined review point. Decide where a person checks output: before payment, before a customer reply, before the board pack. Loosen it only when the numbers support it.
  4. A cost per transaction. Work out the cloud cost per invoice or per query during the pilot and set it against the hours saved. Your CFO will ask for this, so have it ready.

And be honest about where generative AI does not belong. Exact calculations, pricing decisions, and anything that needs a legal signature stay with people. The model drafts and retrieves. Your team decides.

Frequently asked questions

What do generative AI consulting services include?

A generative AI consultant helps you pick a use case worth building and gets your data ready for it. They then build and test the solution on your own documents and connect it to the systems your team uses. Good engagements also include monitoring after launch, because answer quality drifts as your documents change.

How much does a generative AI project cost for a mid-sized company in India?

Cost depends mainly on how many systems the use case touches and how messy the source documents are. A proof of concept on one workflow costs far less than a full rollout, which is why it is the sensible first step. Running costs are mostly cloud consumption per query or per document, so calculate the cost per transaction during the pilot and compare it with the time saved.

How long does it take to move a generative AI pilot into production?

For a narrow, document-based use case, plan on roughly two to three months from discovery to a production release. Use cases that write into an ERP take longer, because integration and access control need testing. If a pilot has been stuck for a quarter, the blocker is usually data ownership or a missing review step, not the model.

Is our company data safe when we use generative AI?

It can be, if the solution runs in your own cloud tenancy with role-based access. Microsoft states that prompts and outputs sent to Azure OpenAI Service are not used to train its foundation models. Design still matters more than the model: limit what each role can retrieve and log every query. If the documents hold personal data, map how it is handled against the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

Do we need clean data before starting a generative AI project?

Not perfectly clean, but owned. A use case can start on imperfect documents as long as someone decides which version is correct and keeps it that way. The proof of concept is the cheapest place to find out how much cleanup a use case really needs before you budget for it.

Can generative AI work in Hindi, Marathi, and other Indian languages?

Current large language models handle Hindi and other major Indian languages well enough for internal assistants, and voice input helps on the shop floor. Accuracy drops with mixed-script text, Hinglish, and technical terms that only exist inside your plant. Test with real questions from your own staff before promising anyone a multilingual rollout.

How do I choose between the top AI consulting companies in India?

Ask each firm to prove one use case on your data before you sign a larger contract, and ask who will do the work. Check whether they have built on the platforms you already run. A firm that agrees to every use case on the first call has not looked at your data yet.
