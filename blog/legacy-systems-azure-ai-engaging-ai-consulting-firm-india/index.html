From Legacy Systems to Azure AI: Guide

Evaluating Azure AI firms in India? AptaCloud provides a buyer’s guide to vet AI consultants, modernize legacy apps, and scale solutions.

Introduction

If you’re a CIO reading this, you’ve probably already made the call to move. The question stopped being whether to modernize a while ago. Now it’s who to bring in, how to structure the deal, and how to avoid the expensive mistakes that keep repeating themselves across Indian enterprises year after year.

Picking the right artificial intelligence consulting firm is genuinely one of the higher-stakes vendor decisions you’ll make this decade. Not because AI is magic. Because it’s sticky. The architectural choices baked into your first proof-of-concept will echo through your Azure environment for years. Get the partner selection wrong and you’re not just losing money on a failed project. You’re paying twice to undo it.

This guide walks through the full engagement arc: from structuring your RFP through to SLA negotiation and ROI projection. We’re starting from legacy app modernization specifically because that’s almost always where the real AI value is buried, even if nobody wants to admit it at the start.

Why Legacy Modernization is the Right Starting Point (Not the End Goal)

Here’s an opinion you won’t hear in most vendor pitches: most organizations are not ready to run Generative AI workloads until they’ve actually sorted out their data plumbing. A company still running SAP ECC on-prem with custom ABAP code from 2009 cannot simply “add AI” on top of that. The data is siloed, unstructured, and often undocumented. Any AI model you bolt onto that stack will inherit every single one of its limitations.

Legacy app modernization isn’t just a prerequisite you sprint through. It’s where the real diagnostic work happens. When we run an Azure architecture review, the findings inside legacy systems almost always dictate which AI services are actually viable in phase one versus what has to wait for phase two or three.

Patterns we see when enterprises first come to us:

  • On-premise SQL Server databases with no ETL pipelines feeding into any kind of centralized data layer
  • Custom .NET 3.5 applications with zero API surface, which makes integration basically impossible without a lift-and-shift first
  • SharePoint 2013 farms still running on-site, quietly acting as unofficial document repositories for data that’s actually mission-critical
  • Manual approval workflows buried in email chains that nobody wants to officially acknowledge are keeping the business running

We’ve watched this fail firsthand when a firm skips the modernization diagnostic and jumps straight to building an Azure OpenAI chatbot on top of messy on-prem data. The chatbot hallucinates. Badly. The business loses faith in AI entirely and the whole program gets set back 18 months. It’s painful to watch, and entirely avoidable.

Start with a structured legacy assessment. Build from there.

Structuring Your RFP When Engaging an AI Consulting Firm

Most RFPs we receive were written for IT infrastructure vendors, not AI partners. They ask about certifications, headcount, and hourly rates. None of that tells you whether a firm can actually deliver a working Azure AI solution inside your specific context.

A better RFP for artificial intelligence consulting services has a few specific elements that separate real partners from resellers with a polished slide deck.

What Your RFP Should Actually Ask

  • Reference architecture for your specific stack: Ask vendors to sketch an Azure reference architecture based on your current environment. Vague responses indicate a consulting-lite firm. Specific responses that name and justify Azure services indicate genuine technical depth.
  • Data residency and compliance posture: Particularly relevant for Indian enterprises. Ask directly how the firm handles DPDP Act compliance and whether workloads will stay within Azure India regions.
  • POC scoping methodology: A serious firm will ask you questions before proposing a POC scope. If they hand you a fixed-scope document without any discovery conversation, walk away.
  • Who actually works on your account: Onshore lead, offshore delivery? What does the escalation path look like when things go sideways? This matters more than it sounds like it does.
  • IP and model ownership: If a custom model or fine-tuned LLM gets built on your data, who owns it? Needs to be contractually explicit. Don’t assume.

One thing we’d add that most people don’t think to ask: request a failed project post-mortem. Seriously. Every firm that’s been doing this long enough has a project that didn’t go as planned. How they talk about that project tells you far more about their maturity than any case study they’ve polished for the sales deck.

POC Scoping: The Most Underestimated Phase

A proof-of-concept is not a pilot. That distinction matters both legally and operationally. A POC proves technical feasibility on a narrow slice of a problem. A pilot proves production readiness across a representative sample of your actual workload. Conflating the two is how budgets blow up and timelines collapse.

For Azure AI engagements, we typically scope a POC across these dimensions:

POC Dimension What to Define Upfront Common Mistake
Data scope Specific dataset, volume, and format Using production data without governance approval
Success metric Quantified threshold (e.g., 85% classification accuracy) Vague “works well enough” criteria
Duration Fixed calendar window, typically 4-8 weeks Open-ended timeline that drifts indefinitely
Azure services in scope Named services: Azure ML, OpenAI Service, Cognitive Services Generic “Azure AI” with no service-level specificity
Exit criteria Go/no-go decision framework agreed in advance POC extends because nobody wants to declare failure

That last row is the one that gets ignored most often. If you don’t agree on what failure looks like before the POC starts, the vendor will never let it fail. It’ll just keep running until your budget runs dry and everyone’s too tired to argue about it.

SLA Negotiation: What Actually Matters for AI Workloads

Standard IT SLAs don’t map cleanly onto AI service agreements. Uptime percentages are almost beside the point. The risks that actually matter in AI consulting engagements are model drift, response quality degradation, and data pipeline failures that silently corrupt outputs over time without triggering any obvious alarm.

When you’re negotiating with an ai consulting firm, push for these specific commitments in writing:

  • Model performance monitoring: Who watches accuracy over time, and what actually triggers a retraining cycle? Monthly review at minimum. Quarterly retraining as a baseline expectation.
  • Incident severity definitions for AI-specific failures: Spell out what a Sev-1 looks like for an AI system. A customer-facing chatbot giving wrong answers is a Sev-1. Treat it exactly like one.
  • Azure cost overrun accountability: AI workloads spike in ways that catch people off guard. Establish who owns optimization and what happens when monthly Azure spend blows past a threshold.
  • Knowledge transfer milestones: Your team needs to eventually own this without the vendor in the room. Build documentation and training obligations into the SLA from the start, not as an afterthought when the engagement is nearly over.

A frank caveat here: no SLA will protect you from a partner who doesn’t actually understand your business domain. Legal language is a backstop, not a substitute for choosing someone who gets your industry. Spend more time on partner qualification than on contract negotiation. Every time.

ROI Projection Model for Azure AI Modernization

Boards want numbers. Here’s a grounded framework we use with clients, broken into three buckets. Actual results vary by industry, baseline maturity, and how well implementation actually goes. These ranges reflect outcomes we’ve observed, not guarantees anyone should be booking into a budget.

Cost Avoidance

Legacy system maintenance is expensive and getting worse year over year. Organizations running on-prem infrastructure typically spend 60-80% of their IT budget on maintenance rather than new capabilities, according to Gartner research. Moving to Azure PaaS services and retiring legacy workloads tends to shift that ratio meaningfully within 24-36 months, though how meaningfully depends a lot on how disciplined the migration is.

Process Efficiency Gains

AI-assisted document processing, approval workflows, and customer query handling can cut manual processing time significantly. Microsoft’s own solution accelerators for intelligent document processing cite 70-80% reduction in manual review time for document-heavy workflows. In our experience with Indian enterprises specifically, finance and procurement functions see the fastest returns on this kind of investment.

Revenue Enablement

This is the hardest bucket to project and the easiest to oversell. We’d be genuinely cautious about booking revenue upside from AI before you have at least 6 months of production data in hand. What you can reasonably model is faster quote-to-cash cycles, better customer retention from improved service, and reduced churn from proactive AI-driven alerts. That’s a reasonable story to tell a CFO.

ROI Category Typical Realization Timeline Confidence Level
Infrastructure cost reduction 12-18 months post-migration High (measurable directly)
Process automation savings 6-12 months post-deployment Medium-High (depends on adoption)
Revenue impact from AI features 18-36 months Low-Medium (highly variable)
Compliance risk reduction Immediate post-implementation High (quantifiable via audit cost reduction)

Build your board presentation around the high-confidence numbers first. Don’t lead with revenue upside. CFOs who’ve been burned by IT project promises will dismiss the entire business case the moment the numbers look speculative, even if the rest of the deck is solid.

What to Expect From an Azure Architecture Review

Before any serious AI engagement kicks off, a credible partner should run an Azure architecture review against Microsoft’s Well-Architected Framework. This is not a sales exercise. It’s a structured assessment across five pillars: cost optimization, operational excellence, performance efficiency, reliability, and security.

For organizations moving from legacy environments to Azure AI, the security and reliability pillars surface the most issues. Identity federation, network segmentation, and data classification are almost always undercooked in the first pass. Always. Microsoft’s Well-Architected Framework documentation is public and detailed, and any partner worth engaging should be genuinely fluent in it, not just nominally aware of its existence.

The review should produce a prioritized remediation backlog. Not a 40-slide deck with amber/red/green status dots and no next steps. If your vendor hands you a presentation with no actionable items tied to specific Azure services and actual timelines, that’s a red flag worth acting on. Among ai services companies operating in the Indian market, the quality of this review varies enormously. Ask to see a sanitized example output before you commit to anything.

FAQ

How long does a typical AI consulting engagement take from RFP to production deployment?

Realistically, plan for 4-6 months from signed contract to first production workload if legacy modernization is part of the scope. The POC phase alone typically runs 4-8 weeks. Organizations with cleaner data infrastructure can move faster, but anyone promising a full Azure AI deployment in 6 weeks without a discovery phase is either oversimplifying or hasn’t thought through what production actually requires. The artificial intelligence consulting firms with the best track records are the ones who deliberately slow down at the beginning so they can move faster later. That’s not a cliche. It’s just how it works.

What Microsoft certifications should an AI consulting firm hold?

At minimum, look for the Microsoft Azure Expert MSP designation or at least Solutions Partner status in Data and AI. These aren’t guarantees of quality on their own, but they do indicate the firm has cleared Microsoft’s technical validation thresholds and maintained a customer success record. For Azure OpenAI specifically, ask whether the firm has direct access to Azure OpenAI Service through an approved Microsoft channel. Not all partners do, and the difference matters when you’re trying to move quickly.

How do Indian data protection regulations affect AI workload design on Azure?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act, 2023) introduces obligations around consent, data minimization, and cross-border transfer of personal data. For AI workloads, this means training data pipelines and inference logs need to be designed with data residency controls from day one, not retrofitted later. Azure India regions (Central India, South India) support data residency requirements. Your ai consulting firm should be able to map your specific workload against DPDP obligations before architecture is finalized. If that conversation is happening after design sign-off, something has gone wrong.
