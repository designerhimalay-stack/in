Generative AI Consulting Services: How Indian Companies Get a Pilot Into Production

A generative AI consultant gets one AI tool working on your company’s own data, then keeps it working after launch. The model is the easy part to buy. The data behind it, and the people who have to trust the answers, take most of the effort.

Somebody at your company has probably tried already. Maybe it was a Copilot rollout to twenty licenses that hardly anyone opens now, or the leave-policy chatbot that answered from a circular withdrawn two years ago. The demo went well. The board nodded. Then the Azure invoice lands. The chief financial officer, the same CFO who signed off the pilot, starts asking what it’s buying. I’d ask too.

What Do Generative AI Consulting Services Include?

I count six pieces of work, and I’d make any firm talk me through all of them before signing. Most proposals are detailed about the build and thin on what happens after go-live. That thin part is where money tends to leak.

The use case needs an owner before it needs anything else. A real person, with a name and a target. “Help the sales team” won’t survive a budget review. “Stop purchase executives digging through four folders for the current approved spec sheet” might.

Then comes the data, which is where I spend most of the first week on any project. Where do the documents actually live? Some of it is in SharePoint. Some sits on a file server under someone’s desk, some in Tally exports, and a surprising amount in a shared mailbox called purchase@. SAP or Dynamics 365 Business Central holds the rest. I also want to know who’s allowed to open each folder, and which half of it nobody has touched since 2021. Nobody enjoys this part. It decides everything that follows.

Next, the architecture. For a company already on Microsoft 365, I’d start with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Your documents get indexed. When someone asks a question, the model answers from those documents and links the one it used. I care more about the opposite case: if there’s no source, the bot should admit it.

If your team spends all day in Microsoft Teams, the bot goes in Teams. An agent built in Microsoft Copilot Studio works too. A separate portal with its own login gets visited twice and forgotten.

Pilots usually skip the last two steps. Testing comes first. My starting point is fifty questions people really ask, pulled from the helpdesk inbox or the plant’s WhatsApp group, with answers your senior people have agreed on. Every prompt edit or model upgrade gets checked against those fifty before it ships. Then there’s running it. Somebody has to read the thumbs-down feedback every week and keep an eye on the bill. If that person doesn’t exist on your side, the project quietly fades by month four.

Why Do Generative AI Pilots Stall?

Usually it’s the data. Sometimes nobody owns the result. Often there’s no way to prove the answers are right. Rarely the model. You can rent a very capable model by the token, but nobody rents you a clean document library.

The Indian numbers are mixed. The headline figure sounds healthy. EY and the Confederation of Indian Industry found that 47% of Indian enterprises had several generative AI use cases live (EY-CII, 2025). Another 23% were still piloting. Look at budgets in the same report, though. More than 95% of firms keep AI and machine learning spend below 20% of their IT budget.

Data is the other drag. An MSL study found 54% of Indian organizations name poor data quality as a barrier (MSL, 2026). Even more of them, 62%, said they lacked the skills or the governance to run AI properly.

For a 400-person company, that budget number is the one that worries me. Second chances are scarce. After one disappointing pilot, the next AI line item gets much harder to defend.

What a stalled pilot looks like

Picture the SharePoint library behind a typical pilot bot. It holds three copies of one standard operating procedure (SOP). SOP_final. SOP_final_v2. SOP_final_USE_THIS. The bot quotes the 2022 one, with total confidence. Someone from quality catches it in the 9:30 meeting, and by Friday nobody is using it. Ask who owns that folder and watch the room go quiet.

What Does a Production-Ready Generative AI Architecture Look Like on Azure?

Six layers. I’ll go bottom up.

Your sources sit at the bottom. SharePoint and file shares, mostly. Then your enterprise resource planning system, plus whatever reports the shop-floor manufacturing execution system spits out.

Preparation comes next, and it’s the layer I’d fight hardest to keep in the budget. Duplicates get deleted. Old versions get retired. Each document gets tagged with its plant and product line, plus an owner and an effective date.

Retrieval runs through Azure AI Search. Permissions come from your Microsoft Entra ID groups. A shift supervisor in Pune opens the bot and sees her plant’s SOPs. Nothing from finance.

For the model itself I’d use Azure OpenAI Service, in an Indian Azure region where the model you want is available. Prompts and answers then stay inside your own tenant, which makes the security review far shorter.

Users never see any of this. They see a chat window in Teams. On top, you need logs and alerts. Set budget alerts in Microsoft Cost Management. Keep the evaluation set running. When the CFO asks how it’s going, you want one page showing accuracy and monthly spend.

Data protection

If staff, dealer, or customer details will end up in prompts, get your legal team into the design discussions in week one, with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 on the table. Keeping processing in Indian regions tends to shorten the legal review.

Why fine-tuning can wait

Someone always asks about fine-tuning in the first meeting, and my answer is always the same: later. Clean, well-tagged content plus good retrieval covers most of what a mid-sized company needs. If retrieval really can’t cope with something, testing will show it, and that’s the moment fine-tuning earns a budget line.

How Long Does a Generative AI Consulting Engagement Take?

Your first use case will most likely take four to eight weeks. It stretches toward twelve when several systems need connecting or the content is in rough shape. Your second use case moves faster, since the data layer, permissions, and testing approach already exist.

What Drives the Cost of AI Consulting Services in India?

Mostly your data and your systems. The model is a small slice of the bill, and Azure usage then runs as its own monthly charge.

Every extra system adds work. A connector into SAP or Business Central sounds like plumbing. The slow part is mapping permissions user by user, so nobody stumbles onto payroll. That old in-house database built in 2011 will need more.

Then look at your content. Scanned PDFs need clean-up before anything gets built. So do duplicate SOPs, and documents nobody owns. Plenty of plants in Maharashtra want Marathi alongside English and Hindi, and every extra language means another round of test questions.

Formal security reviews and penetration tests add weeks. A monthly support arrangement costs more up front than a one-time handover, though in my experience it works out cheaper than rework.

On Azure spend, I’d push for three monthly estimates: a quiet month, a normal one, and a busy one. One figure tells you very little about month-end close.

How to frame it for the CFO

My advice is to ask the CFO for one phase and one use case, nothing bigger. Get the success measure on paper before work starts, along with the name of whoever decides go or no-go.

Timing helps as well. Check where the FY 2027-28 IT budget is. If it’s being drafted between January and March, finish the first phase by then. In April the CFO defends a result, and that’s an easier meeting than defending a forecast.

How Should You Evaluate AI Consulting Companies in India?

Watch how they plan to handle your data and your people. Demos are cheap; month three is where you find out.

Start with the obvious one. Will they prove it on your data before you sign the full project? A serious firm says yes and puts the success criteria in writing.

Ask which of your systems they’ll connect and how permissions carry through. You’re listening for named systems and a concrete answer about Entra ID groups. My favorite question comes next: how will we know the answers are right? If there’s no evaluation set anywhere in the plan, you have your answer.

I’d also want names. Who’s on the project, and how many hours a week will each of them really give it? Ask what the system will cost to run each month. Then ask what you own at the end. The code and the prompts should sit in your tenant. So should the search indexes and the documentation.

Last, ask when they’d advise you against generative AI. Firms with real delivery behind them answer that one quickly.

When Is Generative AI the Wrong Tool?

More often than vendors admit. Take goods and services tax (GST) computation. It needs the same answer every time, and so do bill of materials (BOM) costing and payroll. Use rules and reports for those.

A compressor that’s about to fail shows up in sensor data first. That’s a machine learning job, built on the time-series data in your plant historian.

Some tasks come up twelve times a month. Try a better template before a chatbot. And where a decision carries legal or financial weight, let the model draft it. A person still signs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between AI consulting and generative AI consulting?

They overlap. Broad AI consulting covers work like demand forecasting or spotting defects on a production line. Generative AI work is narrower: tools that read your documents, then answer questions from them or draft first versions for you.

How much do generative AI consulting services cost in India?

Any firm that quotes a fixed price before seeing your systems is guessing. Integration and content clean-up usually decide the number; users, languages, and support move it less. Azure usage comes on top every month. Want a real figure fast? Scope a first phase.

How long does it take to deploy a generative AI solution?

Four to eight weeks is typical for the first one. Heavy integration work can push that to twelve, which is fine if you planned for it. The second one goes faster, since the groundwork is done.

Do we need to move all our data to the cloud first?

No. Only the documents your first use case touches need indexing. The rest can stay put; connectors read SharePoint, file shares, and plenty of on-premises systems where they already are. Starting small keeps the security review manageable as well.

Will Microsoft use our data to train its models?

Microsoft says no. According to Microsoft, prompts and completions sent to Azure OpenAI Service aren’t used to train the underlying models. They also stay inside your Azure tenant. Still, have your security team read Microsoft’s own data and privacy documentation for the service before anyone signs.

Should we hire an AI consulting company or build in-house?

Usually both. Your IT team knows the systems and the people. A consultant brings lessons from other projects and a tested way to check the answers. The condition I’d insist on: by the end, your own people can run it without calling anyone.
